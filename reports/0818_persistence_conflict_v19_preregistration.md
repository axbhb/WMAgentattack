# Persistence and legal-conflict v19 preregistration

Date: 2026-08-18

Status: v2 frozen before repaired manifest construction. Slurm 7115/formal_v1 is retained as an invalid runtime-integrity attempt: six Travel sequences failed before outcomes because the formatter could not serialize a frozen datetime subclass, while three Slack removal steps partially mutated and then raised `KeyError`. It is not a scientific gate result.

v17 established same-root ordinary action-effect diversity, and v18 established same-tool parameter execution boundaries. Neither proves that a successful modification remains observable at a later step or that a second legal update produces a transferable successor distinction. v19 changes only that missing data mechanism.

Each of 12 suite × difficulty task roots receives two three-step branches. Both branches share the exact reconstructed root and first legal write. The persistence control then performs the same read twice. The competing branch performs a second legal write and then the same final read. Banking overwrites a scheduled-transaction subject, Slack appends a second channel message, Travel adds a same-title event at a competing time, and Workspace upgrades a newly assigned file permission.

The v2 repair is label-blind: new tool outputs are converted to JSON-native values before YAML formatting, and the failing Slack removal action is replaced by the documented successful channel-message append/readback pair. Tasks, roots, split, pair count, seeds, budget, metrics, and all thresholds are unchanged. No successful v1 suite result was used to tune a threshold.

The fixed budget is 24 sequences, 144 sequence-step executions, 76 prefix replay calls, and 220 total synthetic sandbox calls. There are no LLM, attack, training, Dreamer, GPU, or real-endpoint calls. The frozen gate requires all steps to succeed, all 12 persistence expectations and all 12 competing-update expectations to match, identical shared first-write states, 12 final semantic and observation differences, all four suites, byte-identical replicas, exact replay, zero leakage, zero runtime failures, and exact budget use.

This pilot validates data identifiability only. A GO authorizes deterministic dataset merging and a separately preregistered tiny task-disjoint model comparison; it does not authorize attack generation or a large model run.
